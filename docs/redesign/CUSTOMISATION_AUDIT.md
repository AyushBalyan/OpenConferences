# Customisation audit

Every place where the product's behaviour, wording or configuration belongs to one organiser, one conference, one country or one deployment, with a verdict for each. This is the Phase 1 input for the Phase 2 work that turns those into settings.

- **Code read:** the `redesign` branch on 2026-11-28: `packages/`, `apps/api`, `apps/worker`, `apps/web`, `apps/mobile`, `infra/`, `.github/` and the root files. `apps/web-v2` and `infra/redesign` are new and not audited.
- **Method:** a pattern search for names, currencies, time zones, fixed periods and switches, then a read of each area, because a rule written into logic matches no pattern. A sample of 24 findings was checked line by line against the code; all matched.
- **Real names:** this document quotes hard-coded names as evidence (decision D11).
- **Companion:** [SETTINGS_AUDIT.md](SETTINGS_AUDIT.md) covers where settings are stored and what production holds. Its IDs start `ST-`.

## Verdicts

- **Core:** stays in code for every conference.
- **Setting:** becomes a validated value with a default, at platform, organisation or conference level.
- **Cut:** deleted. The target column says what replaces it, if anything.

## Needs your decision

Each item names the finding to start from. The proposal is what the tables below assume.

1. **The product's name is the first organiser's name.** "FresiCMT", `app.fresi.org` and `contact@fresi.org` are typed into the legal pages, the home page, the page title and both menus (C-381 and about 40 more). The terms name India as governing law and the privacy page says where data is held. **Proposed:** platform settings for product name, address and support email. Whether the SaaS keeps this name is yours to decide.
2. **One payment account serves everyone.** Razorpay is the only provider, chosen by deployment variables (C-170), called directly in seven places beside the provider interface, with one webhook for the whole deployment. **Proposed:** a payment account per organisation, behind one provider interface (already card P5-3).
3. **Money assumes India and US dollars.** A dollar fee is converted to rupees only for payers in India (C-174), the currency menu offers two currencies (C-451), rupees are the fallback in three places, and amounts assume 100 minor units. **Proposed:** a general "local price for one country" rule per conference, off by default, and no fallback currency.
4. **Fee categories are fixed in the database:** Regular and Student, Early and Regular (C-079). **Proposed:** keep those two by two in the first version, with the student category switchable, and revisit free-form categories later. The findings mark this as a setting; narrowing it is a scope decision.
5. **Accepting a paper always opens paid registration** (C-253, C-187), which fails for a conference with no fees. Unpaid papers are marked withdrawn for non-payment (C-184, C-083). **Proposed:** registration is an optional module; non-payment only flags the paper unless the organiser switches withdrawal on.
6. **There is no time zone anywhere.** Invoice numbers follow India time (C-055) while the invoice prints its date in UTC (C-049); email dates are United States style in UTC (C-022); screens use four different conventions. **Proposed:** a time zone on the organisation, which a conference can override.
7. **Email wording is one organiser's voice.** 14 of the 27 email templates carry tone or policy such as "Dear Professor", "Organising Committee" and the proceedings rule (worst: C-012). The live wording comes from SQL in old migrations, not from the template file (C-020). **Proposed:** neutral platform defaults, with a copy per conference that the organiser edits. Someone has to write the neutral wording.
8. **Reviewer bidding and conflict declaration are off for everyone** (C-243, C-529), and three places ignore the switch (for example C-431). **Proposed:** two conference switches, default off, which matches production.
9. **The review form is fixed:** three scores from 1 to 5 (C-540). **Proposed:** the review form builder already decided in D7.
10. **Signing in accepts every pending reviewer invitation for that address, across all organisations** (C-248). **Proposed:** cut; show the invitations and let the person accept or decline each.
11. **Chairs can refund, extend deadlines and see revenue** (C-196). No one holds the chair role in production. **Proposed:** keep roles in code for now and decide permissions in Phase 3 with the navigation.
12. **Only a platform administrator can create an organisation** (C-301). **Proposed:** self-service sign-up in Phase 5, as planned.

## What this means for Phase 2

1. **An organisation profile and a conference profile.** Neither record holds anything beyond a name today. Identity, time zone, locale, currency, contact address and sender name all need a home.
2. **A settings registry** with a schema and a default for each of the 346 settings found here, most of them small: limits, periods and switches. Many collapse into one setting each; the registry will be far shorter than this list.
3. **Module switches** for registration and payments, student verification, reviewer bidding, conflict declaration, outreach, the bot API and reminder digests.
4. **Neutral email templates with variables,** editable per conference.
5. **A provider-neutral payment layer** with one account per organisation.
6. **Deletions,** in one card, once the owner has agreed the list.
